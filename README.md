# Archive Index Terminal

A desktop reader for **the RCP Archive** — an original institutional-fiction setting written as the internal records of the organization that maintains it.

## The setting

**Restrain. Contain. Preserve.**

The RCP is an organization on Exomania, a world of twelve continents under a single administration, in the year 1472 of its own calendar. It restrains what threatens, contains what cannot be left free, and preserves what must not be lost.

The archive is the setting. There is no narrator and no outside perspective — everything is a document the organization wrote for its own use, in its own voice, for readers who already work there. Anomaly entries record what a thing is and what it does to the people near it. Engineering specifications describe equipment nobody outside the organization will ever see. Reference documents set out standards, classifications, pay, jurisdiction, and the rules the archive holds itself to. Personnel files record who was there.

The documents are written to be read the way real institutional records are read: sideways, in fragments, against each other. An entry states a figure another entry quietly contradicts. A branch enters dissent against a classification and the dissent is retained rather than resolved. Much of what matters is in what the archive declines to explain.

The setting takes obvious inspiration from the SCP Foundation, but the world, the organization, and every document in it are original.

## The application

The Archive Index Terminal is an Electron desktop application that indexes and reads the archive. Documents are held as PDFs and browsed by designation, class, and clearance level, as personnel of the organization would encounter them.
The Terminal includes several features such as changeable themes, clearance gating, a visual reference archive, global statistics and way more. 

## A note on how this was built

I'm new to HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, having previously worked in other languages, and AI was used in building this application while I learn them. I'm working toward being able to build it unassisted.

**Every document in the archive is written by me.** The PDFs are the project; the terminal is how you read them.
